# Baseball card catalogue and preliminary valuation

Reviewed October 7, 2026. Currency: USD.

**35 photographs examined; 315 front-facing pocket entries catalogued.** The cards shown are predominantly 1982 Fleer. The most valuable visible card is the **1982 Fleer #176 Cal Ripken Jr. rookie**, provisionally **$8–15** with the visible corner and edge wear.

**Provisional estimate for these 315 photographed entries sold together: $20–50.** This is a low-confidence selling-range estimate for a worn, incomplete mixed lot, rather than a verified comparable-lot sale. It assumes the photographed entries represent separate physical cards, the Ripken card has no additional serious back damage, and no unusual variant or authenticated autograph is present. The binder itself has no separate assigned value.

## How to read the remaining estimates

Most rows have **category benchmarks**, explicitly marked in the CSV, rather than individually researched sold comparisons:

- Worn commons: roughly **$0–0.25 each** as potential singles; many contribute only bulk-lot value.
- Selected stars and Hall of Famers: roughly **$0.10–1.00 each**, subject to condition.
- Cards with clearly significant damage: roughly **$0–0.10 each**, unless a specifically researched card warrants a different estimate.

These ranges are indicative gross single-card selling possibilities, not guaranteed realizable amounts or net proceeds. Adding the CSV maximum values would assume separate buyers for every card and is **not** the whole-lot valuation. The practical lot range already allows for bulk discounts, condition and the incomplete set.

## Inspection priorities and limits

The Ripken rookie is the first card to inspect front and back outside its binder pocket. Confirm creases, stains, paper loss and whether apparent surface marks are on the card or sleeve. The page photos are sufficient for initial identification but not numerical grading or authentication.

The Pete Rose card in IMG_2643.JPG, bottom left, has visible blue pen marks. It has been treated as marked/damaged; no authenticated-autograph premium is included. Several other cards show substantial creasing or apparent surface damage, flagged where clearly visible. A generic condition note does not certify an undamaged card.

Card numbers were matched to checklists because backs were not supplied. Base versions are assumed; printing variants, Canadian versions, back variations and authenticity have not been established. Rookie flags follow the TCDB checklist convention for 1982 Fleer and identify Tino Martinez's 1990 Score rookie. They do not imply a price premium for every rookie.

Based on the visible wear and estimated values, paid grading is unlikely to be economical for these copies. Closer photographs of the Ripken card could refine its range; the existing photos are enough to begin organizing and pricing the visible collection.
